# [Direction Finding] Frequency Difference Sparse Array Processing with Gridless Arrays [Attached MATLAB Code]

Introduction: Spatial aliasing requirements for DOA estimation are that the frequency should be less than c/2d (velocity c and element spacing d). The above processing provides a fuzzy estimate of DOA. Signals of interest can be out-of-band at higher frequencies, useful for passive array systems. This paper proposes an ungridded sparse difference-of-order (DOA) estimation method that can precisely estimate the DOA of sources outside the aliasing frequency.
This method utilizes pairs (or multiples) of multiple frequencies on the array. Multi-frequency processing leverages more information than one frequency, providing more accurate estimates [1], [2], [3], [4], [5]. A straightforward approach is to process each frequency individually using single-frequency techniques, then average the DOA across all frequencies to obtain the final result. Joint processing of multiple frequencies can outperform single-frequency methods.
Utilizing the spectral characteristics of multi-frequency signals, a DOA estimation method based on a mutually-prime array is proposed. Multiple frequencies are used to fill in virtual co-array elements, thereby improving DOA performance [6], [7], [8], [9], [10]. Mutually-prime arrays use the spacing difference between elements to achieve virtual co-array elements. Similarly, DF uses frequency differences to obtain virtual low frequencies. In a co-array with one frequency, the virtual sensor response is obtained by multiplying sensor data at one sensor with its complex conjugate at another sensor. Joint array methods based on high-order cumulants outperform traditional second-order statistical methods because they suppress noise and achieve virtual array expansion [11], [12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17]. Similarly, in the DF framework, data at one frequency from one sensor is multiplied with its complex conjugate at another frequency. This can lead to higher resolution sensing, aiding in integrated sensing and communication [18].
Multi-Frequency DF (MF-DF) can handle signals above the overlapping frequency falias. This technology utilizes a pair of high frequencies {f, f + f} > falias, where the frequency difference f is below the overlapping frequency f. Assuming that the far-field received on the array is the superposition of plane waves, the frequency difference is obtained by taking the complex conjugate of the data at frequency f and frequency f+. This gives virtual data at reduced frequency f, avoiding spatial aliasing [19], [20], [21], [22].
DF technology is used for array processing and verified through experiments [20], [21], [22], [23], [24], [25], [26], [27]. For DOA, conventional beamforming (CBF) is adopted [20].The DF at frequency f reduces the DF-CBF at frequency f to show results comparable to those without DF at frequency f [23]. For source localization, demonstrations of matched field processing with DF [28], [29], [30] were presented. High-resolution DF-MUSIC (multiple signal classification) [21] and DF grid sparse processing [22] were conducted, but grid-free DF was not derived.
Grid-free sparse beamforming [31], [32], [33] provides high resolution DOA. The sparse DOA method can be extended to multiple frequencies and has been proven to improve performance over single-frequency models [9], [34], [35], [36], [37], [38], [39]. When true DOA is not on the search grid, the grid-sparse method for DOA suffers from baseline mismatch due to the discrete dictionary not representing the data [41], [42], [43]. Baseline mismatch bias can be alleviated by adaptive grid refinement [34], off-grid methods [44] and grid-free methods [40], [41], [45], [46]. Grid-free methods utilize super-resolution mathematical theory and estimate DOA in the continuous angle domain.
We propose the DF-DOA source above the space clutter frequency with ANM. Although the DF method provides DOA estimates without clutter, it generates spurious (artifact) DOA estimates. An ideal direction finding wave arrival direction estimator should estimate both the true wave arrival direction and the artifact wave arrival direction simultaneously, and further analysis should refine these wave arrival directions to the true wave arrival direction. In addition to numerical validation [Section 6], the main contributions are as follows:
1) We introduce DF-DOA and analyze the characteristics of DOA caused by DF, providing insights for integrating the DF concept into state-of-the-art DOA estimators [Section 3].
2) We use ANM [40], [41], [45], [46] to develop high-resolution DF-DOA estimation. This can handle noisy data and provide mean square error estimates for denoising beamforming spectral with atomic norms [Section IV-B].
3) Using multiple measurements at time and frequency. Leveraging multiple equally spaced frequency differences, multiple DF have the same frequency difference and thus the same steering vector. Like traditional multi-snapshot processing (where multiple snapshot data vectors are aligned and processed in a matrix), multiple DF align and process multiple DF data. This method extends to multi-snapshot DF, where all multi-snapshot and multi-DF data are grouped and processed [Section V].
4) For arbitrary intervals of multiple DF, we propose the Multi-DF Spectral Averaging (SA) method, which obtains zero spectra for each DF through solving the multi-snapshot ANM for each DF, identifying the DOA support of each DF sparse signal. Subsequently, the zero spectra on DF are averaged to extract wave arrival direction estimates. This method can be extended to non-uniformly spaced DF and is simple and effective under high Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR). Conversely, under low SNR, multi-snapshot DF is more effective than multi-snapshot DF-SA [Section VI-E].
The article includes illustrations.
Conclusion: This paper derives a high-frequency source DOA estimator without mixing. It utilizes multiple frequency components of the source, especially their frequency differences, to process signals with frequencies below the mixing frequency, thereby addressing spatial mixing. However, this difference frequency (DF) application introduces unwanted (artifact) DOA, necessitating a high-resolution DOA estimator for filtering.
To address this issue, we employ ungridded sparse DOA processing. An ungridded sparse method injects sparsity to obtain sharp beamforming spectrum, enhancing resolution and estimating DOA in a continuous angle domain to overcome grid mismatch. From the perspective of application, various formulas are given for multiple data measurements in both time and frequency under various conditions: multi-shot DFSA and multi-shot DF methods.
Performance indicates that the multi-shot DF performs well, requiring uniformly spaced DF and processing all time and frequency measurement data at low SNR. In contrast, the multi-shot DF-SA, where each DF is processed individually and the average across DF is used for any interval, performs best at high SNR.
